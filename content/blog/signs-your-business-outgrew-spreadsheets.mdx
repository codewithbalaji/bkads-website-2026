Almost every business runs on spreadsheets at some point, and for good reason:
they're free, flexible, and everyone knows how to use them. But there's a moment
when the spreadsheet stops helping and starts quietly costing you — and it's worth
knowing the signs before a small mistake turns into an expensive one.

## 1. Only one person really understands "the sheet"

If a critical process lives in a workbook that only one team member can safely
touch, you don't have a system — you have a **single point of failure** with
formulas in it.

## 2. You're copying the same data into three places

When the same order, customer, or number has to be typed into multiple sheets or
tools, every copy is a chance to introduce an error, and reconciling them later
eats hours.

## 3. Nobody trusts the numbers

The tell-tale sign: people export the data and re-check it by hand because
"the sheet isn't always right." Once trust goes, the spreadsheet has stopped
doing its job.

## 4. You can't answer simple questions quickly

"How many jobs are in progress right now?" shouldn't require someone to stop and
manually tally rows. When basic visibility takes real effort, reporting has become
a task instead of a glance.

## 5. It breaks every time it grows

More rows, more tabs, more people editing at once — and things start to slow down,
lock up, or silently overwrite each other.

> Spreadsheets are a brilliant place to start and a risky place to stay.

## What comes next

Outgrowing spreadsheets doesn't mean a giant, expensive software project. It
usually means a **focused tool that does one job well** — capturing your data once
and giving the whole team a shared, trustworthy view of it. The goal isn't
fancier software; it's fewer mistakes and less time spent wrangling.

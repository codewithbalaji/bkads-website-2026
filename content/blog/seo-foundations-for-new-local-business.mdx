When you're a new business, SEO can feel like a dark art full of tricks and
buzzwords. It isn't. For most local businesses, getting found online comes down to
a few unglamorous foundations done properly — and doing those beats chasing every
tactic you read about.

## Start with the basics that count

- **A fast, mobile-first website.** Most people will find you on a phone. If the
  site is slow or awkward on mobile, nothing else you do will matter much.
- **Clear pages for what you actually offer.** One focused page per core service,
  each with a plain, descriptive title — written for a person first, search engine
  second.
- **Your business details, consistent everywhere.** Name, address, and phone
  number identical across your site and listings. Consistency builds trust with
  both customers and search engines.

## Write for people, not algorithms

The most durable SEO advice is also the most boring: **be genuinely useful.**
Answer the real questions your customers ask. Explain what you do in the words they
would use, not industry jargon.

> The best keyword strategy is usually just describing your service the way your
> customer would search for it.

## Give it time

SEO is a slow compounding investment, not a switch you flip. Early on, the honest
goal is simply to **exist clearly and load fast** — to be findable and credible
when someone searches for exactly what you offer. The rankings follow the
groundwork.

## Don't fall for shortcuts

Be wary of anyone promising instant first-page rankings or selling bulk backlinks.
Shortcuts that game search engines tend to age badly, and cleaning up after them
costs more than doing it right the first time.

## The challenge

MGM Collections sells gold-covering and fancy jewellery, and a large share of its
orders already moved through WhatsApp — customers would browse photos, ask
questions, and confirm an order over chat. Any new website risked breaking that
familiar flow if it forced people through an unrelated checkout.

Behind the scenes, the picture was similarly fragmented: stock, orders, and
billing lived across notebooks and spreadsheets, with no single place to see
what had actually been sold.

## What we built

We designed the platform around the ordering habit customers already had,
rather than replacing it.

- **A full storefront with cart.** Customers browse by category, view
  product pages, and add to cart — shopping online feels just as easy as
  messaging a photo on WhatsApp.
- **WhatsApp checkout, built in.** Customers can move straight from cart to a
  pre-filled WhatsApp order message, keeping the conversation the team is
  used to handling.
- **An admin panel.** Staff manage the catalogue, prices, and incoming orders
  from one dashboard instead of editing files by hand.
- **Billing software.** Orders and billing live in the same system as the
  storefront, so there's one source of truth instead of three separate
  notebooks.

## Why it matters for jewellery retail

Jewellery is a high-trust, high-consideration purchase, and WhatsApp had
already earned that trust for MGM Collections — customers were used to
asking questions, seeing photos, and getting a personal reply before
confirming an order. Forcing a switch to an unfamiliar checkout would have
worked against that trust rather than built on it. Keeping WhatsApp as a
first-class checkout path, instead of routing everyone through a generic
"pay now" button, meant the new storefront extended the buying experience
customers already liked instead of replacing it.

## The outcome

MGM Collections now has an online storefront that feels native to how its
customers already shop, backed by an admin panel and billing system that
replace the old spreadsheet-and-notebook routine. Staff can see stock,
orders, and billing in one place instead of reconciling three sources by
hand at the end of the day.
